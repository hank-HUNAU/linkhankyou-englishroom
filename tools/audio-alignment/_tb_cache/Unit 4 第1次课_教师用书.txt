Istanbul, Turkey
The wonderful city of Istanbul is a great place to 
visit. It is an old city, but there are some modern 
things to see, too. You can visit the beautiful 
Topkapi Palace and then go to Sultanahmet Square. 
On one side of the square is the Blue Mosque, and, 
on the other side, the museum of Hagia Sofia.
Take a boat trip on the Bosphorus – the water 
between the two sides of the city. Then stop and 
have coffee – there are many cafés in the city.
Are there any good places to eat? Of course! There 
are many restaurants, and you can eat great fish for 
lunch or dinner at Kumkapi Market.
ara, Japan
Nara is an old city in Japan, about 
400 km from Tokyo. It isn’t very big – 
about 400,000 people live there – but 
it is famous for many things. Many 
visitors go to see the Todai-ji Temple, 
a huge wooden building. There is a 
statue inside. It’s over 15 metres tall!
In the city there is a park with 
hundreds of deer. There aren’t any 
walls in the park, so the deer can walk anywhere in the city. 
Buy some special food and give it to them – they love it!

Culture notes
Istanbul is the most populated 
city in Turkey and the country’s 
economic, cultural and historic 
centre. Formerly known as Byzantium 
and Constantinople, Istanbul spans 
the Bosporus Strait (which separates 
Europe and Asia). Its commercial 
and historical centre is on the 
European side and about a third of 
its population lives in suburbs on the 
Asian side. Istanbul is the world’s 
fifteenth largest city.
Nara is the adminstrative centre of 
the Nara Prefecture, Japan. It was 
the capital of Japan from 710 CE to 
794 CE. The city contains several 
temples, shrines and ruins, which 
with the Kasugayama Primeval Forest 
form a UNESCO World Heritage Site 
called the Historic Monuments of 
Ancient Nara. 
One of the temples in this UNESCO 
site is the Todai-ji temple. It is a 
Buddhist temple which was first used 
in the 8th century. It was once one of 
the powerful Seven Great Temples in 
the region. Although it was originally 
founded in the year 738 CE, Todai-ji 
was not opened until the year 752 CE. 
Its Great Buddha Hall is home to the 
world’s largest bronze statue of the 
Buddha Vairocana. The temple is a 
listed UNESCO World Heritage Site.
