First Nations is a term used within Canada, for indigenous peoples other than 
Inuit and Métis. These peoples had settled and established trade routes across 
what is now Canada by 1,000 BCE to 500 BCE. People started using the term 
‘First Nation’ in the 1970s to avoid using the word ‘Indian’, which some people 
considered offensive. It became officially used in the 1980s. There is no legal 
definition of the term.
Halloween is a contraction of All Hallow’s Eve and is a celebration that takes 
place in many countries on 31st October, the eve of All Hallow’s Day. Many 
Halloween traditions have pagan roots and originate from ancient Celtic harvest 
festivals such as the Gaelic festival Samhain, which marked the end of harvest 
and the beginning of winter. There is evidence that it has been an important 
date since ancient times. Modern Halloween activities include trick-or-treating 
(or guising) – children going from door to door in disguise – and carving 
pumpkins into lanterns.

Culture notes
The Day of the Dead (in Spanish: 
Día de Muertos) is a three-day 
holiday celebrated by people of 
Mexican heritage. It involves family 
and friends gathering to pray for 
and to remember friends and family 
members who have died. It is not 
viewed as a day of sadness but as a 
day of celebration. The festival can 
be traced back hundreds of years 
to the ancient Aztecs and originally 
took place in summer. After Spanish 
colonisation it became associated 
with 31st October–2nd November.
Lion dance is a traditional Chinese 
dance. It is usually performed 
during Chinese New Year and 
other Chinese festivals. Performers 
in a lion costume mimic a lion’s 
movements to bring good luck and 
fortune. The Chinese lion dance is 
normally operated by two dancers, 
one manipulating the head while the 
other forms the rear end of the lion.
The Colombina is a half-mask that 
is often worn at masked balls. Its 
shape, covering just the eyes and 
top of the nose, allows the wearer 
to eat and drink easily. Many 
other traditional masks have been 
adapted to a similar shape. It was 
designed for the theatrical character 
Colombina, a cheeky servant girl, 
who originated about 1530 in Italian 
Commedia dell’Arte. Her name 
means ‘Little Dove’ and her costume 
included a cap and apron. Unlike 
many of the other characters, she did 
not traditionally wear a mask.
