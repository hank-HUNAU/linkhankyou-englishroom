Statues around the world
‘The Candangos’ were men who travelled from all over 
Brazil, between 1956 and 1960, to build the new capital 
city of Brazil, Brasilia. In 1956, 256 men arrived – but before 
long, there were 30,000, who worked hard to build the city. 
Brasilia opened in 1960, and this eight-metre-high statue to 
remember the ‘Candangos’ is in the ‘Three Powers’ square. 
The two figures are holding digging tools.
Franz Kafka was a writer from Prague. He was born in 1883. 
His books were in German. During his life he was not very 
famous, but now he is. Many of his stories were very strange. 
There’s a very unusual statue of him in Prague. He’s sitting 
on the shoulders of an empty suit!
At the Roland Garros tennis centre in Paris, there is a court 
called Suzanne Lenglen. Lenglen was a famous French tennis 
player, who played from 1914 to 1926. She was the Number 
One player for many years. In front of the court is this statue: 
Lenglen is jumping with a racket in her hand, and she is 
wearing her famous white dress and headband.

One day in 1965, a bus driver in Alabama, in the US, asked Rosa 
Parks to give a man her seat – because he was white, and she 
was black. She said ‘No, ’ and the police decided to take her away. 
It was a very important moment in the history of black people’s 
rights in the US. The statue shows Rosa sitting on a rock, wearing 
the same clothes as that day in 1965.

Os Guerreiros (The Warriors), popularly known as Os Candangos, was crafted 
by Brazilian sculptor Bruno Giorgi in 1959. The huge bronze sculpture is eight 
metres tall and can be found in Brazil’s governmental plaza, Praça dos Três 
Poderes (Three Powers Plaza), in the capital city of Brasilia. The sculpture is seen 
as the symbol of the city.
Suzanne Lenglen was a tennis player from France. She was born in 1899 in Paris 
and began playing tennis at the age of 11. She was coached by her father and 
quickly stood out as a child prodigy. At only 15, she won her first major title at 
the 1914 World Hard Court Championships. She was ranked number one in the 
world between 1921 and 1926, and won 21 Grand Slam titles, eight of which as 
a single player. Unhappy with her low income, she gave up her amateur status 
and became the first women’s tennis player to turn professional. She was one 
of tennis’s biggest stars in the 1920s and is often regarded as the first female 
athlete to become a global sport celebrity.

Culture notes
Rosa Parks was an activist from 
the United States. Born in 1913 in 
Alabama, she became famous during 
the civil rights movement in the 
1950s and 60s and is best known for 
her central role in the Montgomery 
bus boycott. On 1st December 1955, 
while travelling on a segregated bus 
in Montgomery, she was asked to 
give up her seat to a white passenger 
after the whites-only section was 
full. She refused. Following her 
arrest, the black community in 
Montgomery boycotted the buses 
for 381 days. Parks’ act of defiance 
and the Montgomery bus boycott 
became important symbols of the 
Civil Rights Movement. She became 
an international symbol of resistance 
to racial segregation.
Franz Kafka was a German-speaking 
writer from what is modern-day 
Czech Republic. He was born in 
1883 in Prague and is regarded 
as a major figure of 20th-century 
literature. His best-known works 
are The Metamorphosis, The Trial 
and The Castle. In his will, Kafka 
asked his friend and fellow novelist 
Max Brod to burn all unpublished 
manuscripts, but Brod edited them 
and had them published. Kafka’s 
writings are about the strange and 
terrifying world around him, which he 
struggled to understand. As a result, 
people describe frightening and 
bizarre situations as ‘Kafkaesque’. 
Franz Kafka died from tuberculosis in 
Austria in 1924.
