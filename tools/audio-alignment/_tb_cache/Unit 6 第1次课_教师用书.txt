Activity idea Extension
Have a reading race. Divide the class into pairs and tell students to find as many 
different verbs in the article as possible in two minutes (not including to be / to 
have/have got). When time is up, students count up how many different verbs 
they have found. Ask the pair with most verbs to read them out and make a list 
on the board. Ask other pairs to add to the list if possible.
There are 19 different verbs: like, goes, buys (doesn’t buy), drinks, does, asks, 
says, (don’t) eat, (don’t) worry, thinks, wants, opens, takes, puts, sees (see), 
feels, tell, gives, have.
Culture notes
In England, all infant school pupils 
(children in Reception and Years 1 and 
2) in state-funded schools are able 
get a free lunch at school. In Scotland, 
all children in the first three years 
(Primary One to Three) get them. 
Outside of these year groups, some 
children are still entitled to free school 
meals, depending on what social 
benefits the family receives or if the 
family is on a low income. Although 
not technically at school anymore, 
students between 16 and 18 are also 
offered free lunches in some parts 
of the UK, depending again on their 
parents’ circumstances.
In addition to school lunch, all 
children aged between four and 
six attending a state-funded infant, 
primary or special school in England 
are entitled to receive a free piece 
of fruit or a vegetable each school 
day. This is usually handed out just 
before the mid-morning break and the 
scheme aims to increase awareness 
of the importance of eating fruit and 
vegetables and to encourage healthy 
eating habits that can be carried into 
later life.
Schools in the UK provide pastoral 
care for children who need support 
in a wide variety of situations, for 
example: bereavement, parental 
separation, domestic abuse, poverty, 
caring responsibilities, a toxic home 
life.
