Language note like / don’t like + -ing
Students may form questions such as Like you swimming? Point out that we use 
the auxiliary do to form sentences in the present simple (Do you like swimming?).
Language note Collective nouns
Collective nouns are ones that describe a group of things or people as a unit. 
They can take a singular or plural verb form and this often depends on whether 
we are thinking of the group as an impersonal unit, which is singular, or as a 
collection of individuals, in which case we use the plural verb form. Compare the 
following examples:
• Tom’s family is wealthy – we use the singular is because we’re talking about 
one family, one unit.
• Tom’s family are teachers – we use the plural verb because we are talking 
about a group of separate individuals, all of whom happen to be teachers.
