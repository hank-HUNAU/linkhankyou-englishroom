Listening: Richard Ward on travelling well
Hello again.
Right.
He’s visited more than a hundred countries.
He’s taken thousands of photos and published them in travel magazines.
He’s got a very popular travel blog.
But he’s taken a break today to talk about how to travel well.
Richard Ward, thank you for coming today.
Thanks for inviting me.
So you’ve just completed your tenth year of travelling.
That’s right.
Ten years without spending more than a week back home.
Well, my parents’ home.
I’ve never had my own home.
And to celebrate your ten years, you’ve made a list of top things to do when you travel.
That’s right.
And I’m hoping to publish them in a book later this year.
Have you ever written a book before?
No, I haven’t.
This will be my first.
How exciting.
Now, on your website, there are twenty tips for travelling.
We can’t talk about all of them so can I ask you, what’s your number one tip?
Well, that’s difficult.
My number one?
Hmm.
OK.
I’m going to say: ‘Get off the beaten track’.
I mean, try and go to places that other people don’t go to.
Of course, it’s great to see all those famous places that everyone wants to see.
But if you explore a bit further, go to other places, you’ll discover much more interesting things, you know?
On my blog I talk about an important difference: the difference between travellers and tourists.
A tourist always stays on the beaten track.
A traveller likes to leave it.
Of course, you might get lost …
And have you ever got lost?
Oh yes, hundreds of times.
In fact another of my tips is ‘try to get lost’!
It’s part of the fun of travelling.
If you get lost sometimes, you can find amazing things, great people.
OK, let’s talk about things to take with you.
Another tip you have is: ‘always take a scarf’.
Why?
Well, a scarf is a really useful thing.
You can wrap it around you to keep you warm or to keep the sun off.
You can carry things in it, too, or tie things together.
Well, I want to talk some more, Richard.
